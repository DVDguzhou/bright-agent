# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary: advice-seekers — people who want to consult someone with real lived experience on a specific topic (career change, study abroad, parenting, niche skills). They arrive at the discovery feed, browse agent profiles, and start a paid conversation. They are Chinese-speaking, mobile-first, and motivated by authenticity over credentials.

Secondary: agent creators — people who have accumulated knowledge in a particular domain and want to make it accessible and monetizable. They manage their profile, knowledge base, and earnings through the dashboard.

## Product Purpose

A marketplace where real people publish AI agents trained on their own life experience, and seekers pay to consult them. Success looks like: a seeker finds someone whose background matches their exact situation, has a conversation that feels genuinely personal, and comes away with advice they couldn't get from a generic AI.

## Brand Personality

Warm, human, trustworthy. The tone of a well-edited personal essay or a long interview with someone who has genuinely lived through what they're describing. Not a product catalog, not a social feed, not a productivity tool — a curated library of real human experience.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS (Notion, Linear): neutral grids, productivity-tool polish, no soul.
- Social media (Xiaohongshu, Instagram): follower counts, likes, engagement metrics as the visual language. Real-experience credibility should not look like influencer content.
- AI startup branding (ChatGPT, Claude): dark chat UIs, gradient logos, "intelligence" abstraction. The product should emphasize the human behind the agent, not the AI powering it.

## Design Principles

1. **The human is the product.** Every surface should foreground the creator's story, background, and voice — not platform chrome or AI capability.
2. **Earned trust, not asserted authority.** Credibility comes from specific detail (school, job, location, experience count) never from badges or superlatives.
3. **Editorial restraint.** The magazine aesthetic is directionally right but should be pushed toward substance: generous white space, typographic hierarchy, meaningful hierarchy — not decoration for its own sake.
4. **Mobile conversation as the core.** The chat interface and the mobile experience are the moments that matter most. Everything else is a path there.
5. **Show the experience, don't label it.** Let the agent's words, topics, and seeker reviews speak. Minimize platform-level claims.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG audit planned. Apply sensible defaults: minimum 4.5:1 body text contrast, sufficient touch targets (44px minimum), Simplified Chinese as the sole locale.
